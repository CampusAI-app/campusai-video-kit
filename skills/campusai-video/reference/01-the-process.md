# The process

This is how Jacob makes videos, and it is the order to work in.

## 1. The idea

What the video is about, in a sentence or two, and which real part of the product carries it. If
there are a couple of good directions, say so and say which one you would pick and why. Agree the
idea before going further.

## 2. The flow

How the video moves from start to finish, and what each part is hitting. Beat by beat: what is on
screen, roughly how long, and the point that beat lands. This is the shape of the video, not the
finished copy. Agree the flow before building.

Things that come up here every time:

- **Open on the product, not on a logo.** Frame one is the cover in a feed. A black or near-black
  first frame reads as "not loaded" or as an ad.
- **One point per beat.** If a beat is hitting two things, it is two beats.
- **Every claim should be checkable in the demo.** If a beat says the product does something, a
  viewer should be able to open the demo and see it.

## 3. The point, and the call to action

Why the video exists. What the viewer should know or feel at the end, and what they should do next.
Every beat in the flow should be building toward this, so if a beat is not, it goes. Ask Jacob for
the current call to action rather than guessing one, because it changes with where the launch is.

## 4. Build

Now make it. Raylight or Remotion (see the table in `SKILL.md`), with the real screens from the
demo (see `02-the-real-product.md`).

Build one beat, look at it, then the next. Do not build the whole video before looking at any of it.
In Raylight, write a one-paragraph storyboard from the agreed flow before the first edit, then render
stills after each scene. In Remotion, render frame-exact stills of each beat before believing any
timing.

## 5. Feedback

Show it, get feedback, change it. Walk `09-before-its-finished.md` before showing, so the feedback is
spent on the idea and the feel rather than on things a checklist would have caught.

When feedback comes in:

- **Separate what is said from where it sits before cutting anything.** Two intro lines once got cut
  because they played over a black screen before any product appeared. The lines were fine. Moving
  them into a caption band under the product fixed it and kept copy that was already liked.
- **When the same note comes back twice, stop changing the thing you already changed** and ask what
  the problem actually is. The second time usually means the first fix was aimed at the wrong thing.
- Hand each version over with what changed, what you are unsure of, and anything left out on purpose.

Nothing public goes out until Jacob has cleared it.

## Working with Claude through all of this

- Talk in plain words. Describing the situation ("Instagram feed, second-years, the week before the
  career fair") gets better work than describing the task.
- Never take the first version. Ask what is weakest about it.
- When Claude states something about the product confidently, check it against the demo. It does
  get the product wrong, and it does not always know when.
