# Getting better every video

This skill is a starting point, not a finished rulebook. Two habits keep it improving: looking for
better tools and parts before settling, and writing down what each video taught so the next one
starts from there instead of from zero.

Everything learned lives in the `learning/` folder at the top of the workspace (next to `CLAUDE.md`),
not inside this skill. Reinstalling the skill replaces its folder, and the learning has to survive
that.

## 1. Look for something better

The rule on this team: do not rebuild what already exists, free beats paid, look to the community
first. That applies to the tools themselves, not just the parts inside a video.

**When to look:**

- **You hit a wall that costs money or caps the work.** A licence fee, a plan limit, a watermark.
  That is the moment to check whether a free tool does the same job. (Example: Remotion needs a paid
  licence for CampusAI, and a search turned up HyperFrames, which is free for any company and built
  for AI agents.)
- **You are about to write something from scratch.** A text reveal, a transition, a counter, a
  caption style. Someone has almost always published it.
- **You are doing the same manual step for the second time.** A repeated step is a tool or a script
  waiting to exist.
- **Once at the start of a new kind of video.** A format we have not made before is worth five
  minutes of looking at how others make it.

**How to look,** cheapest first: `learning/shelf.md` (it may already be answered), then GitHub with
the `gh` commands in `08-tools-and-parts.md`, then the connectors (DeepWiki to ask about a repo,
Context7 for current docs, Inspo for references).

**How to judge what you find:** licence, last updated, stars as a rough filter, and whether it
overlaps something we already use. Read it on GitHub first. Then try it in a scratch folder, never
straight in the studio. Then say what you found and what you would do, and let Jacob decide. Do not
switch tools quietly.

**Record it either way** in `learning/shelf.md`: adopted, worth watching, or rejected, with the
reason and the date. A rejected tool with a reason saves the next person the same search. A rejection
is not permanent either. Recheck anything older than a couple of months, because tools change.

## 2. Learn from every video

**Before starting a video,** read the `learning/` folder:

- `taste.md` what Jacob has liked and rejected, and why. This outranks general good taste.
- `lessons.md` what earlier videos taught.
- `videos.md` every video made so far. Start from the closest one rather than a blank page.
- `shelf.md` tools and parts already found.

**After each round of feedback,** write it down before moving on:

- In `taste.md`: what he liked or rejected, in his words where possible, and what it says about his
  taste in general, not just about this video.
- In `lessons.md`: anything that broke, surprised you, or took longer than it should have, and what
  to do differently next time.

**When a video is done,** add it to `videos.md`: the idea, the flow, the point, where the files are,
what worked, and what you would change.

**Let it build on itself.** When the same lesson shows up twice, it is no longer a one-off. Move it
into the reference file it belongs in (a timing rule into `04-the-grammar.md`, a capture trap into
`02-the-real-product.md`) and mark it in `lessons.md` as moved. That is how this skill gets sharper
instead of just longer. If something in a reference file turns out to be wrong, fix it there and note
the correction in `lessons.md`, so nobody relearns the wrong version.

**Check facts that go stale.** Prices, plan limits, which demo pages exist, what the product can do.
Anything in this skill with a date older than about a month gets checked before a video depends on
it.

## 3. Share it back

Every few weeks, or after a big lesson, send Jacob the `learning/` folder. What one person learns
should reach everyone who makes CampusAI video, and he folds it into the next version of this kit.

## Keep it light

A line or two per entry is plenty. The point is a record the next video can actually use, not a
diary. If writing it down takes longer than a minute, it is too long.
